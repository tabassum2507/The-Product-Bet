---
title: "OKRs and KPIs are not the same tool"
date: 2026-08-05T20:40:00+05:30
tags: ["product-management", "analytics"]
description: "I used OKR and KPI interchangeably for longer than I want to admit, until a quarterly review made the gap obvious."
---

We walked into a quarterly review with an OKR that read "increase activation rate to 40%," and someone on the leadership side asked, reasonably, whether that was the objective or the key result. I said both, because in my head at that point they were the same thing wearing different labels. They aren't, and the confusion cost us a slightly embarrassing ten minutes of the review re-deriving what we actually meant.

A KPI is a metric you track on an ongoing basis to know whether the business, or a part of it, is healthy. It doesn't expire, it doesn't need a deadline, it's just a number you watch — churn rate, activation rate, average handle time. An OKR is a time-boxed commitment: an objective, which is qualitative and a little aspirational, and key results, which are the specific measurable things that would have to be true for you to say the objective was achieved. A KPI can become a key result inside a specific OKR cycle, but not every KPI deserves to be one, and not every key result should become a permanent KPI just because it was useful for a quarter.

The mistake in that review was that we'd taken a KPI we already track continuously, activation rate, and just bolted a target and a quarter onto it and called the whole thing an OKR without an actual objective behind it. What were we trying to become true about the business that "40% activation" was evidence of? Nobody had written that down, so the number floated with no story attached, and the first hard question exposed that.

Where I think this matters practically: KPIs are for monitoring, they should trigger investigation when they move, not action on their own. OKRs are for focusing effort somewhere specific for a defined window, at the cost of not focusing effort somewhere else. If you treat your entire KPI dashboard as this quarter's OKRs, you haven't actually chosen anything, you've just restated everything you already track and called it a strategy. I did this exact thing with the churn-rescue agent's numbers early on — proposed an OKR set that was just our five standing KPIs with target values attached, no prioritization, no story about why those five mattered more this quarter than last.

The tradeoff I've come around to is that a good OKR set is deliberately incomplete. It should leave out things you know matter, because naming everything as a priority means nothing actually is one. That's uncomfortable to defend to a stakeholder whose favorite metric didn't make the cut, and I don't think there's a clean way around that discomfort, just a clearer story for why this quarter's few key results are the ones that move the objective, and a KPI dashboard sitting quietly underneath watching for anything the OKRs didn't anticipate.
