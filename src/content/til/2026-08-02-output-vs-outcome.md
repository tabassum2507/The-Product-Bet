---
title: "TIL: The difference between output and outcome — and why my roadmap items were all outputs"
date: 2026-08-02T22:10:00+05:30
tags: ["til", "product-management"]
description: "I mapped every item on our roadmap and realized every single one was an output, not an outcome."
---

I was walking a new hire through our roadmap and she asked what outcome each row was supposed to produce. I went down the list, ship the CSV export, ship the bulk-tagging feature, ship the new onboarding flow, and realized I was answering with more outputs, not outcomes. An output is the thing you built. An outcome is the change in user or business behavior you built it to cause. "Ship bulk tagging" is an output. "Reduce time-to-organize-a-workspace by half" is the outcome bulk tagging was supposed to serve, and it was nowhere on the roadmap, just implied and never written down.

That's a bad habit hiding in plain sight, because a roadmap full of outputs still looks like a real plan, it has rows and dates and owners. What it doesn't have is any way to tell whether shipping the thing actually worked, because there's no outcome attached to check it against. We'd been measuring "did we ship it" as success, which is a much lower bar than "did it change anything," and nobody had noticed because the two questions get answered on different timelines, shipping is obvious the day it happens, whether it worked takes weeks to know and by then the team's already on the next output.

I went back and added an outcome line to every roadmap item, even a rough one. A few of them I genuinely couldn't fill in, which was its own answer.
