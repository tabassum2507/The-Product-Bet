---
title: "TIL: STAR answers fall apart at the R — I was describing what I did, not what changed"
date: 2026-09-08T23:30:00+05:30
tags: ["til", "interview-prep"]
description: "In a mock interview I kept describing what I did instead of what changed, and the gap was the R in STAR."
---

Mock interview today, standard "tell me about a time you drove a difficult decision" question, and I ran through Situation, Task, Action fine, clear and specific. Then I got to Result and just kept talking about what I did some more, phrased slightly differently. The interviewer stopped me and asked, plainly, "and what changed because of it." I didn't have a crisp number ready, just more description of the action dressed up as an outcome.

That's apparently a common failure in STAR answers and I'd never noticed it in myself until it was pointed out in the moment. Result isn't a recap of the action, it's a separate claim: what metric moved, by how much, over what period, and ideally, how you know it was this decision and not something else happening at the same time. "I pushed the team to prioritize the retention fix" is action, restated. "Thirty-day retention went from 61% to 68% over the following quarter" is a result.

I went back through three of my go-to interview stories after that session and found the same gap in two of them, action bleeding into a vague sense of success with no actual number attached. Fixing it is just homework now, go find the real numbers before the next interview, not during it.
